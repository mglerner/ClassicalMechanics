## Skips

- Tides (class 26, Taylor 9.2): no physical demo shows the second (far-side) bulge
  for the right reason. The CU Boulder "Tidal Distortion" item is an elastic loop on a
  basketball, a cartoon of the answer, not a demonstration; water-tray "tide models" get
  the far bulge from centrifugal-force hand-waving that Taylor 9.2 explicitly replaces
  with the -mA argument. Use the helium-balloon demo to set up "-mA everywhere" and
  then draw the tidal field.
- Hohmann transfer / changing orbits (class 25, Taylor 8.8): no tabletop demo; the
  spandex sheet has the wrong force law and friction, and an air-table puck on a
  tether is a Hooke force. It is a simulation topic; see orbit-catchup-sim.
- Kepler's laws with a physical orbit (classes 23-24): no apparatus gives a real
  1/r^2 orbit; the spandex sheet is useful only as the "orbits that do NOT close"
  counterexample above.
- Reduced mass / two-body CM motion (class 23): the usual demos (two air pucks
  joined by a spring, dumbbell thrown across the room) are intro-level and too slow
  for the payoff at this level.
- Shive (Bell Labs) wave machine for the many-coupled-oscillator limit (Ch 11): Taylor
  11 stops at three coupled pendula (11.6) and the general case (11.5); the wave
  machine is a waves-course demo and would cost 5-10 min for an idea the class does not
  use.
- Physical driven damped pendulum (class 36): available apparatus (PASCO CI-6689A) is
  not Taylor's equation and period doubling is hard to get reliably live; see the
  simulation entry.
- Poincare sections / bifurcation diagrams (Taylor 12.6-12.8): simulation only, and
  likely out of scope for the class list as given.
- Liouville's theorem as such: no direct physical demonstration exists; the Couette
  "unmixing" cell is an honest analog only for area preservation and only if it
  already exists at Smith.
